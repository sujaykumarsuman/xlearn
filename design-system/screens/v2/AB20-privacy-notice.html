xLearn

Privacy notice

Version 1Effective 1 Dec 2026

This notice explains what xLearn keeps about you, why it keeps it, where it lives, and the choices you have. You accept it when you join, and again whenever it changes.

1Who runs xLearn

xLearn is a personal project run by one person — its owner — not a company. The owner builds and runs the service and is responsible for the data in it. For any question or request about your data, email the owner — the same address as Request an invite on the sign-in page.

2What xLearn stores

Only what the course needs to work for you:

  • Your account — your email address, display name and username; a hash of your password (never the password itself); and the id of your GitHub account, if you connect one.
  • Your settings — your time zone, study budget and reminders.
  • Your learning — your attempts and the code and answers you submit, their timings and grades, your revision schedule, your mistake journal and your mock-interview scores.
  • Your coach — your coach chats, and the AI keys you add yourself, stored encrypted.
  • Your answers when you join — your region, your confirmation that you're 18 or older, the version of this notice you accepted, and your AI and profile choices, each with the date you made it.

xLearn sets one cookie, to keep you signed in. There are no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Server logs record technical details such as the time and the IP address of a request, to keep the service running and to stop abuse; your code, answers and AI prompts are never written to them. Nothing is sold, or shared for marketing.

3How AI is used

xLearn uses AI in two separate ways.

xLearn AI — only if you opt in

When you join you're offered two choices, both off: "xLearn AI reviews my graded work" and "…and also reviews my passing solutions for improvement notes". If you turn them on, the work you submit is sent to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude models, to suggest a grade and feedback. It is processed outside India. Anthropic may keep it for up to 30 days — content its safety systems flag may be kept for up to 2 years — and never uses it to train its models. Answers to behavioral questions are sent only after a separate opt-in in Settings. With xLearn AI off, you grade your own work and nothing is sent. xLearn itself never uses your work to train AI models.

Your coach — your own key

The coach uses an OpenAI or Anthropic key that you add yourself. Your messages go to that provider under your own account with them, and their terms apply. xLearn stores your key encrypted, and keeps your coach chats so you can come back to them.

4What's public

If you pick a username, you get a public profile at projects.sujaykumar.dev/xlearn/u/<username>. It shows your display name and username, a rough time-zone band (never your exact zone), the month you joined, and stats for the courses you leave visible — for example problems solved, your streak, activity and how many mock interviews you've done. It never shows your code, answers, feedback, mistakes or notes, and never your email. Courses are visible by default, except behavioral ones. You can hide any course, or make the whole profile private, in Settings → Profile & visibility.

5Where your data lives — and what could be lost

xLearn runs on a single rented server at a hosting provider, and everything above is stored there. Your password is stored only as a hash, and your AI keys are encrypted.

xLearn keeps no off-node backups — no copy on any other machine. The only safety net is a weekly image of the whole server that the hosting provider keeps. If the server or its disk fails, up to about 7 days of your data may be lost: everything since the last weekly image.

6Your choices

  • xLearn AI — both choices are off unless you tick them. Turn either off any time in Settings, and grading falls back to you straight away; copies Anthropic already holds expire on its schedule. Behavioral answers need their own opt-in.
  • Your profile — make it private, or hide any course, in Settings → Profile & visibility.
  • Your data — to ask what xLearn holds about you, or to have something corrected, email the owner.
  • Erasing your account — in Settings → Sign-in & security → Erase account. If your account can't erase from the web yet, email the owner and it is erased for you. Erasing removes your sign-in, profile and settings, all your learning, mocks and progress, your public profile, your coach chats and your saved AI keys from every part of xLearn within minutes. A few traces remain for a while:
    • your username is held for 60 days, so no one can take it over at once; then anyone can claim it;
    • a weekly server image kept by the host may still hold your data for up to about 7 days, and a pre-release snapshot for up to 1 day. If one of them is ever restored, the owner re-runs the erases made since it was taken;
    • some internal event records keep a random account id, and older sign-up records also keep your display name, until the owner removes them on request;
    • if xLearn AI was on, copies Anthropic holds expire on its schedule, within 30 days (2 years for flagged content).

7Age

xLearn is for people 18 or older. You confirm this when you join. If the owner learns that an account belongs to someone younger, the account is erased.

8Changes to this notice

Every version of this notice is numbered and dated; this is version 1, effective 1 Dec 2026. When it changes, the top of this page says what changed, and you're asked to accept the new version before you carry on using xLearn.

§Terms

By using xLearn you agree to these terms, alongside the privacy notice above.

  • xLearn is invite-only: you can join only through an invite link from the owner.
  • One account per person.
  • Invites are single-use and meant for the person they were sent to. Please don't share or post them.
  • The owner may suspend an account that breaks these terms or puts the service or other learners at risk.
  • xLearn is provided as is, without warranty of any kind. It's a personal project, and it may change, pause or stop.
  • xLearn's course content and code are MIT-licensed.

Questions about these terms? Email the owner.

  • The last section of the same page (anchor #terms), under the same version and date; accepting the notice at AB19-F9 covers it. Contents lists it after §8.
  • Invite-only and single-use: D33, ADR-0033 §5. MIT: R-CT1 (the private eval pack is not public content, so it isn't mentioned). No waitlist, no web admin (D33).
